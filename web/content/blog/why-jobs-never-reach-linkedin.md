When you search for jobs on LinkedIn or Indeed, it's easy to assume you're seeing the market. You're seeing the part of it that companies chose, and often paid, to put there.

Every role starts somewhere else first: the company's own careers page.

## Every careers page runs on an applicant tracking system

When a company clicks "Careers" on its website, the page you land on is almost never built by the company. It's run by an **applicant tracking system** (ATS): software that hosts the job listings, collects applications and lets recruiters move candidates through interviews.

Three of them run a large share of tech and startup hiring:

- **Greenhouse**, used by many mid-size and large tech companies
- **Ashby**, popular with newer startups
- **Lever**, common across startups and scale-ups

Each company gets its own board, such as `jobs.ashbyhq.com/linear` or `boards.greenhouse.io/stripe`. When a recruiter opens a role, it goes live there immediately.

## Why roles skip the job boards

Getting a role from the careers page onto LinkedIn or Indeed is a separate step, and it often doesn't happen:

- **Promoting a job costs money.** LinkedIn charges to promote listings. A company hiring for twenty roles might pay for the five that are hardest to fill.
- **Some roles fill from referrals.** If the team expects to hire someone they already know of, there's no reason to advertise widely.
- **Small companies don't bother.** A ten-person startup posts on its Ashby board, tweets it once, and gets enough applicants.
- **Sync delays.** Even when roles are sent to job boards automatically, they can arrive days later.

The result is a large number of real, open roles that only exist on the company's own page. You find them only if you already know the company and check its board.

## Why being early matters

Applications are read most carefully in the first days after a role opens. After a week or two, many popular roles have hundreds of applicants, and new ones mostly sit in a pile.

That's why Unlisted only keeps roles from the past week. Older roles aren't useless, but your time is better spent on the ones where your application will actually be read.

## How Unlisted searches the source

Greenhouse, Ashby and Lever all publish each company's board in a form software can read. Unlisted uses that:

1. **It keeps a list of more than 7,700 companies** and which system each one uses.
2. **Every hour, it reads the boards**, picks up new roles and notices closed ones.
3. **It cleans up each role**: where the job can be done from (remote anywhere, a region, or one country), how much experience it asks for, and the pay, when the posting states it.
4. **Every listing links to the employer's own posting.** You apply on the company's page, not through Unlisted.

Each job page also checks, live, that the role is still on the company's board, so you don't apply to something that closed an hour ago.

## Making it work for you

- **Search everything at once.** The [main search](/) covers every board, with filters for remote, country, experience and pay.
- **Look at companies, not just roles.** Each company has a page with all its open roles, such as the [companies hiring this week](/companies).
- **Follow searches.** Save a search and get new matches by email or notification after each hourly update.
- **Let it rank for you.** [For you](/for-you) orders every role by the roles you want, your skills and, if you add it, your CV.

Job boards are still useful. But the careers page is where hiring actually starts, and for many roles it's the only place they exist.
