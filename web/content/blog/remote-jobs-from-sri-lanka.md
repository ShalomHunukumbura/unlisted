If you've searched for remote work from Sri Lanka, you know the pattern. A role says "Remote", you read the whole description, you start the application, and on the last screen it asks for your US work authorisation.

That's not bad luck. Most remote jobs are remote **inside one country**. This guide is about telling the difference quickly, and about where the roles you can actually take come from.

## Most remote jobs are remote "here"

Companies hire remotely, but they still have to pay people legally. Hiring someone in a new country means payroll there, tax there and employment law there. So most remote postings quietly mean "remote, as long as you live where we already have a company".

Unlisted sorts every remote role into one of three kinds, from what the posting says:

- **Remote · anywhere**: no country or region named. These are the ones to look at first.
- **Remote · a region**: open to a region such as APAC or EMEA. APAC usually includes Sri Lanka; EMEA usually doesn't.
- **Remote · one country**: "Remote, US only", "Remote (Canada)". Unless it's your country, skip it.

In a typical week, only around one in six remote roles is open from anywhere. The weekly [hiring report](/blog) has the exact numbers.

## How to read a posting in ten seconds

Before you read the job itself, look for these:

1. **The location line.** "Remote, US" or "Remote - United Kingdom" settles it.
2. **"Must reside in" or "authorised to work in".** Usually near the end, often in the benefits section.
3. **Benefits that only exist in one country.** A 401(k), US health insurance or "PTO in line with UK law" means the role is tied to that country.
4. **Time zones.** "Overlap with PST" isn't a location rule, but 9am to 5pm Pacific is 9:30pm to 5:30am in Colombo. Be honest with yourself about it.
5. **Contractor or employer of record.** "Contractor", "EOR", "via Deel" or "via Remote.com" is good news: it means the company already pays people in countries where it has no office.

## Who hires from anywhere

The roles open to Sri Lanka tend to come from the same kinds of companies:

- **Companies that are distributed by design**, with no main office and staff across many time zones. Their careers pages usually say so up front.
- **Companies that pay through an employer of record**, which lets them hire almost anywhere without opening an office.
- **Developer tools, open source and crypto companies**, where teams were global from the start.
- **APAC-focused companies** hiring for regional support, sales or operations roles.

When a company posts one role open worldwide, its other roles often are too. If you find one, look at the company's whole board.

## Where these roles appear first

Every company's careers page runs on an applicant tracking system, usually Greenhouse, Ashby or Lever. That's where a role is posted first. Some reach LinkedIn or job boards days later; many never do, because the company doesn't pay to promote them.

Unlisted reads more than 7,700 of those careers pages every hour and keeps the past week, so a role usually shows up within an hour of being posted. Being early matters: the first few days are when applications get read most carefully.

## Applying from Sri Lanka

A few things make the application easier for the person reading it:

- **Say where you are, and that you know what it means.** One line: "I'm in Colombo (UTC+5:30) and can overlap with your team's mornings."
- **Be ready for a contractor setup.** Many companies hiring abroad start with a contract. Know how you'd invoice and get paid, for example through Wise or Payoneer, before the call where they ask.
- **Make your time zone a strength.** For APAC-facing roles, or teams that want coverage while the US sleeps, UTC+5:30 is an advantage, not a problem.

## A quick checklist

- Location line says anywhere, APAC, or names Sri Lanka
- No "must reside in" another country
- No country-only benefits (401(k) and the like)
- Time zone overlap you can actually keep
- Ready to work as a contractor if that's what they offer

The good roles exist. There are just fewer of them than the word "remote" suggests, and they go to the people who see them first.
